# Exploration scenery

The interactive exploration screen draws on 151 recovered official backgrounds under `original/`. The 139 H5 raster images retain their original source bytes; twelve Flash background layers are registered static SVG exports. Existing official creature portraits remain separate interactive actors. Some current official sources contain later redesigns; exact 2009 artwork is not claimed.

`original-manifest.json` records source URLs, SHA-256 digests, dimensions, registrations and extraction details. `src/domain/world/map-art-assets.json` projects only offline paths and cache identities into the game; `world.ts` assigns the matching regional background, or the parent background for a custom rendezvous/training area. `tools/assets/download_original_maps.py` fetches every map ID that the world sources and `data/habitat-regions.json` name, except a region's own art that file records as unused; it writes only new files, records H5-unavailable IDs under `failures`, and adds each delivered background to that projection. A habitat region in that file shows its own background where the official H5 art and road mask exist and the art fits the stage, and its parent map's otherwise; its `ownArtwork` records why. These own backgrounds came after the V44 HD restorations, so they show at native resolution; `tools/verification/hd-assets-v44.py` allows native rasters only for maps added after V44 that are habitat maps. `tools/assets/extract_map_floors.py` derives the source floors in `src/features/world/map-sources.ts` from the cached road masks. The seven earlier authored SVGs and their `manifest.json` remain legacy fallback assets. No promotional screenshot is used as a shipped background.

Region coordinates, walking bounds, routes, suggested levels, and navigation order are explicit remake choices. Every planet and SPT entry remains available without a progression lock. The navigation preference is stored separately from gameplay saves and does not alter save import or export.

## Integration and verification

`WorldMap` fills the shell's scene viewport and accepts `s`, `c`, optional `onNavigate(page)`, optional `focusSpecies`, optional `travelTo: {planetId, regionId?, requestId?}`, and optional `onInteract({kind: 'species', species})`. The exported `WorldAtlas` accepts `onTravel({planetId, regionId})` and optional `currentPlanetId`; the shell hosts it in a separate overlay. Increasing `requestId` permits repeated travel to the same destination.

Exploration has compact in-scene location text, adjacent-region exits, local creatures and station facilities. The top map tool is the single general navigation entry; the redundant right-hand transporter and in-scene route button have been removed. Ground clicks and arrow keys move the robot. Creature, NPC and exit clicks initiate movement; creature or facility dialogue opens only after the walking timer reaches the destination. Dialogue offers explicit challenge, details and leave actions. Atlas travel resets the scene position without animating a walk across unrelated regions. There is no permanent detail column, external planet-card strip, large page heading or footer panel. The shell keeps the map mounted while hiding it for battle, preserving position and dialogue selection.

Placed wild species open their habitat. Placed boss species open their SPT location. Other catalog species open a recruitment commission in the transport cabin and use the existing controller's encounter routing. The atlas exposes 55 destinations from the official client table's first five galaxies, the historically qualified small-Phantom route, and three bases, with adjacent modeled regions connected inside the scene. All 48 current SPT encounters have explicit destination placements. All 129 modeled regions use official backgrounds: 123 exact regional IDs and six custom areas reusing their parent background. Walking geometry remains a compact reconstruction rather than the complete original collision map. Wild populations retain the existing habitat policy. Bosses retain their anytime availability. Creature art uses the shared `CreatureArt` component and the SVG-rendered viewport calibration described in `tools/assets/README.md`. Source identities and qualification details are recorded in `design/notes/challenge-location-expansion-sources.md` and `design/notes/original-map-asset-delivery.md`.

Focused component-browser checks at 1280 by 720 confirmed ground movement, delayed creature dialogue, travel through the swamp exit, the complete species-70 body in Helka, complete dialogue art, boss details, the enabled unplaced-species recruitment action, and the atlas's seven destinations and 21 region actions. The scene had no document scrolling at 1280 by 720 or 800 by 600. Tall, wide, small and evolved silhouettes were reviewed separately through the shared renderer. TypeScript compilation passed. These presentation checks do not independently certify battle outcomes or reward mechanics. Shell-level battle return and HUD integration are verified separately.
